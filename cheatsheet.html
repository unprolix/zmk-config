jjb keymap — what to remember

Goal of this page: drill the things you keep forgetting. Reference-style detail lives in config/jjb.keymap and friends. Open this when you want to learn or break a habit.

1 — Modifiers, three flavors

Same modifier, three ways to invoke. Pick the one that fits the moment.

You want…Hold-style (intuitive)Sticky-combo (when hold misfires)
CTRL+<key>hold A (left index), then key on rightLH0+LH1 thumb pair (NEW) — primary one-shot
SHIFT+<key>hold E (left middle), then key on rightI+E middle row → tap a 2nd time = caps_word, 3rd = CAPS lock
ALT+<key>hold I (left ring), then key on rightE+A middle row
GUI+<key>hold inner thumb (under SPACE)LH1+LH2 thumb pair (NEW)
CTRL+SHIFT+<key>(awkward — two same-hand HRMs fight prior-idle)H+I adjacent middle row (NEW position) — for terminal copy/paste!
If your CTRL keypress is misfiring during fast typing, switch to the thumb-pair combo LH0+LH1. It's a single-press one-shot CTRL — no HRM timing involved. Same for GUI: LH1+LH2.

(Right-hand mirrors all of the above. S = RCTRL, T = RSHIFT, R = RALT, N = RGUI; combos exist on right home row too.)

No expansion in the table for that letter? Arcane falls back to &sk LSHIFT (or caps_word on a 2nd consecutive press). So tap-a-letter-then-thumb is also how you get a sticky shift — useful right after typing.

If you find yourself spelling out a word in the table, you forgot. Drill the ones that match your most-typed words. The user said "I'm not using these as much as I should" — this is the page that fixes that.

3 — Brackets are a top-row roll

Two adjacent top-row keys at the same time, on the same hand. The bracket matches what's between them visually.

Left top row outer→inner: ~ F Z U Q ⌫. Right top row inner→outer: ⏎ W D L B ;. Pair adjacent positions, same hand:

Roll left top-row (outer→inner)…GetRoll right top-row (inner→outer)…Get
~+F<⏎+W>
F+Z(W+D)
Z+U[D+L]
U+Q{L+B}
Q+⌫escB+;_
You forgot these. Try saying the open/close bracket as a single roll — the muscle memory comes back fast.

5 — Stop typing things you don't have to

Adaptive shortcuts (no extra key — the keymap inserts the rest)

  • After U, the Q key produces K (so the rare K-after-U bigram doesn't reuse the middle finger)
  • After ., the J key produces ../ (parent dir shorthand)
  • After I, the H key produces 't (currently behaves as a "can't"-tail; the I'd / I'll / I've contractions go via the dedicated combos below — see "Single-fire macro combos")

Single-fire macro combos

  • RB4+RB5 (.+/) → ../ (parent dir)
  • RB1+RB4 (C+. diagonal) → .com
  • LB5+RB5 (outer bottom corners) → switch to QWERTY
  • LM3+RB2 = "I'm "; LM3+RT3 = "I'll "; LM3+RT2 = "I'd "; LM3+RB0 = "I've "

Mod-morphs (shift the punctuation)

  • shift + ' → -  ·  shift + " → _
  • shift + . → :  ·  shift + , → ;
  • em-dash via 3-key combo LB2+LB1+LB0 (left bottom-outer) or RM3+RM4+RM5 (right home-row outer)
Subtle: tapping the comma key alone produces ", " — comma and a space. (Behavior comma_semicolon in hierophant.dtsi.)

7 — When typing feels wrong, try this

"thisi s" instead of "this is" — you rolled the SPACE-thumb into 'i'. The hold-tap on the inner thumb defers SPACE emit to release. Workarounds: deliberately separate space from the next letter, or transition to the new thumb-pair GUI combos (LH1+LH2 / RH1+RH2) so the inner thumb can be demoted to bare SPACE.

shift+' didn't produce " — you used the same-hand shift HRM (E on LM3) for the left-hand SQT (LM0). Same-hand HRMs don't fire. Use the opposite-hand shift (T on right) or the I+E sticky-shift combo first, then tap '.

HRM modifier didn't fire — your previous keypress was within HRM_PRIOR_IDLE (150ms), which disables the HRM and forces tap. Pause briefly before the modifier-letter, or fall through to the sticky-combo path (LH0+LH1 for CTRL, LH1+LH2 for GUI, etc.).
